# Sound recordings

The site's own audio material, for the sound features on `/handles/`. Two
canvases: one recording in one file, and four recordings tiling one timeline.
Unlike the images beside it under `material/`, this is not generated by a
script — the media are their own artifacts, and what follows is where they came
from and what was done to them.

**Retrieved:** the cylinder 2026-09-09 and the marches and portrait 2026-09-09,
all from Wikimedia Commons.
**Total:** 5 audio files (3.9 MB), 1 image (92 KB), 1 waveform (43 KB).

## Why the Cookbook's own audiovisual recipes are not used here

Most of them are far too large for a page that fetches the feature showing. The
Donizetti recording behind recipes 13, 26 and 229 is 933 MB; recipe 64's first
act is 545 MB; recipe 17's video is 177 MB and recipes 3 and 219's is 79 MB. The
one that is small enough — recipe 74, a 4.4 MB Europeana newsreel — carries the
page's video features, and is left on the Cookbook's server because its rights
statement reserves all rights.

## The recordings

### The cylinder

Public domain: recorded in 1888, and published on Commons under `PD-US-expired`
/ `PD Edison Records`. Credited on Commons to the National Park Service (Thomas
Edison National Historical Park) and the Internet Archive.

| File                   | Recording                                                                                                                                         | Duration |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| `media/lost-chord.mp3` | Arthur Sullivan's _The Lost Chord_, on an Edison "Perfected" cylinder, 1888 — piano and cornet, one of the earliest surviving recordings of music | 2:06.8   |

### The marches

Public domain as works of the United States Government: performances by the
United States Marine Band, of marches by John Philip Sousa. Four of the
shortest of the band's Sousa recordings on Commons, because these are the bytes
a reader pays for a demonstration of chapters, and four marches make the point
that four longer ones would not make better.

| File                              | Recording               | Duration |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------- |
| `media/columbias-pride.mp3`       | _Columbia's Pride_      | 1:08.4   |
| `media/quilting-party.mp3`        | _The Quilting Party_    | 1:29.6   |
| `media/guide-right.mp3`           | _Guide Right_           | 1:31.9   |
| `media/pet-of-the-petticoats.mp3` | _Pet of the Petticoats_ | 1:48.8   |

**Transcoded**, every file, because the Commons originals are 320 kbps stereo
and Ogg Vorbis is not playable everywhere: each was converted from its Commons
original with
`ffmpeg -ac 1 -ar 44100 -c:a libmp3lame -b:a 64k -map_metadata -1`. Nothing was
cut, and the durations above are the transcoded files' own — the manifest
declares them, and the four marches' `#t=` segments are laid end to end from
them, so re-transcoding at another bitrate means regenerating `manifest.json`'s
durations to match.

## The waveform

`waveform/lost-chord-waveform.dat` is `audiowaveform` v1 binary, 8-bit, mono,
256 samples per pixel — 21,835 points over the 2:06 recording:

```
audiowaveform -i media/lost-chord.mp3 -o waveform/lost-chord-waveform.dat -z 256 -b 8
```

The canvas links it in `seeAlso` under the BBC waveform profile, which is one of
the two signals `@triiiceratops/plugin-av` looks for (`waveformLink.ts`).

Only the cylinder has one. The plugin's companion rules make a waveform and
cover art exclusive: a canvas core paints a companion Canvas into gives the
stage no lanes to draw peaks in, so the bare canvas gets the waveform and the
one with a cover does not.

## The image

`images/sousa.jpg` is a Library of Congress photograph of John Philip Sousa in
band uniform, dated 1900 (LCCN 92502141, digital id `cph.3c04651`), resized to
1000px on the long edge at Q80:

```
convert <original>.jpg -resize 1000x1000 -quality 80 images/sousa.jpg
```

It is the `accompanyingCanvas` of the four-march canvas. No tile pyramid is cut
for it: core makes one appropriately-sized request for a companion canvas and
never deep-zooms it.
